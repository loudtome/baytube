Sasha's Notes

Field advice from someone who's actually run the A.T.
Notes from Sasha, uploaded ahead of the relay — pace expectations, gear, navigation, permits, closures, and wildlife.

Pace

Running the A.T. is a lot harder than most trails and absolutely more difficult than any road. Train on single track and get your vert in! My road speed is 10 min/mile, my trail speed is 12 min/mile and my A.T. speed is 15–20 min/mile. Yes, I'm old and slow, but I'm just showing you how much slower the A.T. is — plan accordingly.

Lighting

If you do this relay you will need two lights. One to use and one as a back up. You do not want to be on the trail in the dark and not be able to see well. I like the belly lights, but the headlights are great and some people advocate for handheld. Your call.

Navigation

Zillions of people hike these trails without getting lost, but it can happen! "Inchworm" got lost for almost a month on the A.T. before succumbing. And navigating is decidedly harder in the dark. Remember to look for the white blazes. Side trails are blue or yellow but not white. You should see a white blaze at least every 5 to 10 minutes. I encourage using the FarOut App to track your location. It works in airplane mode and won't wear your battery down. Familiarize yourself with the app before you start. If you are unsure where you are, stop, think and check the app and look in front or behind for white blazes. (But don't go in the wrong direction just because there is a white blaze behind you.) If you think you are on the right trail go another 5 minutes and if you don't see a white blaze, backtrack until you do. Also, I always read the mileage/direction posts on the A.T., just to make sure.

Trekking Poles

I'm also a huge advocate of using trekking poles. They make uphills and downhills easier, but mostly they have kept a stumble from becoming a fall and potential 911 call a lot of times. A lot. It's a good idea to practice with them beforehand.

Park Entrance Fee

The National Park requires hikers and trail runners to have paid the entrance fee. I have the lifetime old persons pass that allows entry for up to 15 persons in the vehicle. This holds for an annual pass or any pass, so shouldn't be a problem for our relay team. Also, organized races are not allowed in the Park, but this is just you and your friends enjoying the trails in a decidedly not organized non event and is exactly what the Park is for.

Skyline Drive Closures

Skyline Drive closes for bad weather or road obstructions. Check their website for announcements before starting. And the campgrounds and stores close end of October — check the NPS website if you're close to those dates and you care about access, especially if you plan on meeting a runner in one of those locations.

Segment Length & Water

I've tried to keep the segments under a max of 10 miles. I think if you have a few snacks and a running vest with water you should be fine. But if you do this in the heat of summer, you might want a water filter and look closely at FarOut to know where water can be found. I always bring a snack or two, and I like Saltsticks, but any kind of salt will do.

Wildlife

It is true there are bears in the Park. The Park recommends fighting back if attacked by a black bear (they actually have signs saying so!), so be sure to practice your kicks and punches — No, this is not a concern! Bears are not looking for trouble and want nothing to do with you. There are rattle snakes and copper heads. You should seriously look where your feet are going and don't step on one. They, too, want nothing to do with you, so just don't step on one and don't freak out over this.
